Guide: what every option means

The map explorer shows, for one obs class at a time, how well the observations fit a reference (climatology or the model) in each geodesic bin: 10,242 cells of roughly equal area covering the globe. You choose what to show with the rows of buttons; the maps redraw at once. Everything here is computed from the same saved results as the report’s figures.

The rows of buttons

RowOptionWhat it means
SourceCTD_WOD, PFL, …The obs class (instrument type): CTD and bottle casts, gliders (GLD), ice-tethered profilers (ITP), seal tags (MEOP), moorings (MRB), Argo floats (PFL), XBTs. Each is analyzed on its own.
Cost typevs_climReference = the WOA23 climatology (1991–2020 average) at the profile’s location, month and depths.
vs_modelReference = ECCO v4r6, from its monthly-mean T and S sampled at the profile’s grid cell, month and depths (approximate: not the model’s value at the profile’s exact time). No model values after 2025.
VariableT, STemperature or salinity. S is grayed out for classes without salinity (XBT).
CostunsplitThe cost per datum: (ΔV/σ)², where ΔV is observation minus reference and σ the assumed error of the observation. 1 means “one assumed error away”; 4 means two. Averaged over the bin’s points in the selected depths and years. “True weights”: the area factor γ and the spatial scaling factor sf are left out, so this is purely misfit relative to σ.
anomaly (split)The same, after removing the bin’s long-term mean misfit at each depth level: it measures how well the variations in time are fit, ignoring a steady offset. This is the first term of ECCO’s split cost (cost_profiles.F). The long-term mean is computed from this obs class’s own points, all years.
mean term (split)The second term of the split cost: the steady offset itself, (mean observation − mean reference)², weighted by the bin’s combined error w̄ = 1/max(s/n, s/9), s the mean of σ² over the bin’s n points (as cost_profiles.F). One value per bin and level; averaged over the selected levels. Defined over the whole record, so the time selection is off. Its values are typically much larger than the per-datum cost, since it doesn’t shrink with the number of points: compare maps of the mean term with each other, not with the other parts.
# pointsThe number of observations (profile values) in each bin for the selected depths and years: the “n” behind the averages. Bins with very few points give noisy averages.
Diagnosticmean bias (obs − reference)Not a cost term, so it has its own row. The average of the signed misfit, observation minus reference, over the bin’s points in the selected depths and years, in °C (T) or psu (S). Red: the observations are warmer or saltier than the reference; blue: colder or fresher. The color scale is symmetric about zero. Use it with the time range to see whether a steady offset is constant or drifting (model drift, a change in instruments or regions, or ocean change against a fixed climatology). It is related to the split’s mean term (which is this offset, over the whole record, squared and weighted), but here it keeps its sign, has units, and can be limited to any years.
Depthall depthsAll depth levels together (each point counts once, so the upper ocean, which has more and closer-spaced levels, dominates).
single levelOne of the 97 standard depth levels (2 m to 6000 m); pick it in the list.
100 m bandsAll levels in a 100 m range (0–100 m, 100–200 m, …). Below 2000 m the levels are 200 m apart, so some bands are skipped.
15 groups / 30 groupsThe 97 levels split into 15 or 30 consecutive groups of about equal numbers of levels.
flagged spikesLevels where the mean cost per datum of this class stands out: above 0.5 and at least 1.4 times the median of the levels 300–1000 m above and below it (levels with at least 30 points). The list gives the level’s value and that median, and whether it was flagged in the unsplit or anomaly cost. Use it to find where a peak in the report’s cost-vs-depth plots (analyses 2 and 9) comes from.
(list)The level, band, group or spike to show.
Timeall yearsEvery year of the record. Fast: uses a small all-years summary.
the two handlesA range of years: drag the left handle for the first year and the right one for the last. The map shows the average over every point in those years. Choosing years downloads that class’s year-by-year data in 5-year pieces, only those the range needs (up to about 20 MB per piece for Argo, a few MB for most classes), so the first range in a period can take a few seconds; after that, changes are quick.
single yearLocks the handles together, so dragging moves a one-year window.

The maps and the color bar

Maps: global (equal-area projection, starting at the western tip of Australia), and north and south polar stereographic views. White = no data in that bin for the selection; gray = land. Hover a bin to see its number and value.

Color bar: ticks give the values; by default the scale runs from 0 to the 98th percentile of the bins shown, and values above it get the top color (so a few extreme bins don’t wash out the rest). max: set the top of the scale yourself, e.g. to compare two selections on the same scale (the scale otherwise changes with each selection). auto: back to the 98th percentile. log color scale: spreads small values out (covers three powers of ten below the max).

Reading it

  • Values near 1 mean the misfit is about what the assumed errors σ allow; much larger values point to a poor fit, a too-small σ, or bad data. With few points (# points) a single bad profile can dominate a bin.
  • If unsplit is high but anomaly is low in a region, the misfit there is mostly a steady offset (see the mean term). The mean bias shows that offset’s sign and size; compare early and late year ranges to see if it changes.
  • The time range shows whether a high cost is persistent or limited to some years (e.g. one cruise or float).
  • Data notes, open decisions and corrections are on the report’s Notes & errata page.

Precision: values are stored to 3 significant digits. Time resolution: one year. Bins are the same geodesic bins as the report and the ECCO split cost.
