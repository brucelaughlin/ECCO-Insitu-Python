Guide: what every option means

The map explorer shows, for one obs class at a time, how well the observations fit a reference (climatology or the model) in each geodesic bin: 10,242 cells of roughly equal area covering the globe. You choose what to show with the rows of buttons; the maps redraw at once. Everything here is computed from the same saved results as the report’s figures.

Averaged over the bin’s data in the selected depths and years. “True weights”: the area factor γ and the spatial scaling factor sf are left out, so this is purely misfit relative to σ.
anomaly (split)The same, after removing the bin’s long-term mean misfit at each depth level: it measures how well the variations in time are fit, ignoring a steady offset. This is the first term of ECCO’s split cost (cost_profiles.F). The long-term mean is computed from this obs class’s own data, all years.
mean term (split)The second term of the split cost: the steady offset itself, (mean observation − mean reference)², weighted by the bin’s combined error w̄ = 1/max(s/n, s/9), s the mean of σ² over the bin’s n data (as cost_profiles.F). One value per bin and level; averaged over the selected levels. Defined over the whole record, so the time selection is off. Its values are typically much larger than the per-datum cost, since it doesn’t shrink with the number of data: compare maps of the mean term with each other, not with the other parts.
# dataThe number of observations (profile values) in each bin for the selected depths and years: the “n” behind the averages. Bins with very few data give noisy averages.
Misfit, σmisfit (ref − obs)Not a cost term. The average of the signed misfit, reference minus observation, over the bin’s data in the selected depths and years, in °C (T) or psu (S). Red: the reference (climatology or model) is warmer or saltier than the observations; blue: colder or fresher. The color scale is centered on zero; type a value in “max ±” to set its range. Use it with the time range to see whether a steady offset is constant or drifting. It is related to the split’s mean term (this offset over the whole record, squared and weighted), but here it keeps its sign, has units, and can be limited to any years.
normalized misfit (ref − obs)/σThe same, with each value divided by its assumed error σ first (true weights, no γ or scaling factor): ±1 means one assumed error. Its square, averaged, is the unsplit cost. Same red–blue scale centered on zero.
σ (assumed error)The mean assumed error of the observations, σ = 1/√w with the true weights, in °C or psu: the errors the costs are measured against. Where the cost is high, check whether σ is small there.

Loading

While data are downloading or the maps are being recomputed, the maps are dimmed and a box in the middle says what is happening, with the download progress in MB. While you drag the time handles, the year label follows at once and a blue note says the maps will update when you let go; they recompute a moment after you stop. The first time range in a period downloads that class’s year-by-year data (tens of MB for Argo on a slow connection can take half a minute); after that it is kept, and changes take well under a second.

The maps and the color bar

Maps: global (equal-area projection, starting at the western tip of Australia), and north and south polar stereographic views. White = no data in that bin for the selection; gray = land. Hover a bin to see its number and value.

Color bar: ticks give the values. The scale runs from 0 to the 98th percentile of the bins, and values above it get the top color (so a few extreme bins don’t wash out the rest). It is fixed for a view: computed once from all years for the current obs class, cost type, variable and depth selection, and kept while you move the time range, so differences between years are real and not rescaling. Unsplit and anomaly share one scale (same units), so switching between them is a fair comparison. It is recomputed when you change the class, cost type, variable or depth, and for the mean term, number of data, misfits and σ, which have their own scales. The note next to the color bar says which applies. max: set the top of the scale yourself (kept until you press auto), e.g. to compare two depths on the same scale. auto: back to the automatic scale. log color scale: spreads small values out (covers three powers of ten below the max).

Below the maps: the depth profile and the clicked bin (since 2026-11-02)

Depth profile of the selection: for the current class, cost type, variable, cost part and years, the mean over bins at each depth level (red), as analysis 02 draws it, with the number of bins per level (gray, log scale, right). Same bins at every depth adds a blue curve over only the bins that have data at every level of the chosen range (default 2000–2800 m). A jump in the red curve that the blue one does not show comes from which bins are counted changing with depth, not from the water. (Example: PFL vs V4r6 mean term at 2200–2400 m: red 4.9–5.6, blue 2.3–2.4; most floats stop at 2000 m, so those levels hold far fewer bins, mostly subpolar North Atlantic ones.)

Clicked bin: click any bin on a map. The panel shows its center, and per depth level (the selected levels) the number of data, the unsplit and anomaly costs, the mean term, and the mean reference − observation, the normalized misfit and σ (all years); a bar chart of the selected cost part per year (for the mean term: reference − observation per year, since the mean term itself has no years); and a link that opens the in situ data viewer on that bin, zoomed in to the individual profiles, at the same class, reference (climatology or model), variable, depth and years.

Region: is it here? (since 2026-11-03)

Click select region, then drag a box on any map: its bins join the region (outlined in purple); shift-drag removes bins, shift-click adds or removes one bin. The region is a set of bins, so it stays when you change class, cost type, variable, depth or years. With a region:

  • the depth profile shows the region (purple; faint where it has fewer than 5 bins), the rest of the world (gray) and all bins (light red). If a feature disappears from the rest-of-the-world curve, the region accounts for it. With “same bins” on, both curves use only bins with data at every level of the range.
  • a table: the region’s share of the bins with data, of the data, and of the summed cost (or count), at the selected depths and years, and how many times its share of bins that is. (Not shown for the misfits and σ, which don’t add up.)
  • per year: the region (bars; faint with fewer than 5 bins) against the rest of the world (line).
  • the region’s ten largest bins; click a row for that bin’s breakdown and the link to its profiles.

Caution: a region drawn around the darkest bins will always carry a large share. Judge it by its share of the term relative to its share of bins and data, and by the rest-of-the-world curve, not by the share alone.

Peak: where does a bump in the depth profile come from? (since 2026-11-03)

Choose a depth range around the bump: it is the shaded band on the depth-profile plot. Set it with the “from / to” menus, or on the plot: drag the band’s top or bottom edge (the purple handles), drag inside it to move it, or drag across an empty part of the plot to draw a new band. Choosing a range does nothing by itself; then press Investigate the peak in this range (or follow “investigate” under analysis 02 in the report). A short plain-language summary comes first. The page runs three fixed steps and reports the numbers; it does not interpret them:

  1. Peak: the level with the largest value of the curve in the range, its number of bins, and the levels just above and below.
  2. Same bins: the same over only the bins with data at the peak and both neighbors. The part of the peak’s excess over its neighbors that disappears is due to which bins are counted changing with depth; the rest is in a fixed set of bins.
  3. Where: the top 10 % of bins at the peak, grouped into clusters of neighbors (bin centers within 600 km), each with its share of bins and of the summed value. The largest cluster becomes the region (the region panel and its tables fill in); click another cluster to switch. A cluster holds only the high bins; widen the region by dragging on the maps.

Cluster names are descriptive: the smallest Natural Earth sea, gulf or bay containing most of the cluster’s bin centers, otherwise a coarse hand-drawn box (e.g. “subpolar North Atlantic”, “Brazil Basin”) or the ocean; the center coordinates follow.

Platforms in the region (in the region panel): the region’s platforms (Argo floats by WMO number, ITPs, seal tags, moorings; for WOD ship classes the ship or platform code, which is coarser), with profiles, years, share of the region’s value and mean obs − reference. The explorer stores costs per bin, level and year, not per profile, so a platform’s share is apportioned: each (bin, year)’s value is divided among its platforms by their numbers of profiles there (with a value at the selected depths). It is exact where a platform is alone in a bin and year. Since 2026-11-04 the table is computed profile by profile instead, from the in situ data viewer’s profile details: each profile’s own cost, or for the mean term w̄·(mean misfit)·(its misfit)/n, so the parts add up to the term (a profile pulling against its bin’s mean gets a negative part). Rebuilt this way the mean term agrees with the analysis to 0.14 % of its total (PFL vs model, 2400 m; the rest is the details’ rounding to 0.001). The apportioned split is used only if the details can’t be loaded. “show profiles” opens the in situ data viewer with that platform’s profiles ringed in cyan.

Reading it

  • Values near 1 mean the misfit is about what the assumed errors σ allow; much larger values point to a poor fit, a too-small σ, or bad data. With few data (# data) a single bad profile can dominate a bin.
  • If unsplit is high but anomaly is low in a region, the misfit there is mostly a steady offset (see the mean term). The misfit (ref − obs) shows that offset’s sign and size; compare early and late year ranges to see if it changes.
  • The time range shows whether a high cost is persistent or limited to some years (e.g. one cruise or float).
  • Data notes, open decisions and corrections are on the report’s Notes & errata page.

Precision: values are stored to 3 significant digits. Time resolution: one year. Bins are the same geodesic bins as the report and the ECCO split cost.
