Notes & errata

⚠ The Black Sea is left out of the model costs. Profiles in the Black Sea, Sea of Azov and Sea of Marmara are not counted in any cost against V4r6 or V5r1 (every analysis, map, explorer view and the chatbot); the climatology costs and analysis 01 keep them. Why: ECCO V5r1’s Black Sea is far too warm at depth (about 13.6 °C at 1,200 m against about 8.9 °C observed), so it alone was 25 % of the CTD cost against V5r1 from 0.3 % of the bins (Argo: 6 %), and it hid the rest of the comparison. V4r6 has no Black Sea, so leaving it out keeps the two models comparable. A choice for this demo (2026-11-08): to keep the Black Sea, set MODEL_EXCLUDED_AREAS = () in compute.py and re-run the analysis.

1. Reading the figures

  • Analysis 1 is a sanity check on the data itself, e.g. it would show a double potential-temperature conversion.
  • Split vs unsplit: where split is much lower than unsplit, most of the misfit is a steady offset at that location (bias) rather than variation over time. Split can exceed unsplit in individual months where the anomaly runs against the long-term bias.
  • Analyses 2 and 9: a level with large cost means persistent disagreement there, from the ocean or from data problems. With true weights, values near 1 match the assumed errors σ; values well above 1 suggest σ is too small at those depths (or bad data).
  • Analysis 3 vs 4: 3 is per datum, so it tracks fit quality; 4 is a sum, so it also rises and falls with the number of observations (top panel of 4).
  • Analysis 5 is dominated by the upper ocean. The vertical sum counts every depth level equally, and the 97 levels are 2–5 m apart near the surface (23 levels in the top 100 m) but 200 m apart below 2000 m (6 levels in 2000–3000 m). The thickness-weighted version (second analysis 5 figure) gives each level its layer thickness instead, so a 200 m deep layer counts about 70 times a 3 m surface layer.
  • Analysis 11 (supplemental) splits the data by year. Data from after the climatology’s years (WOA23 1991–2020) can’t have been used to build it, so a much lower vs_clim cost for the older data than for the newer would suggest the climatology fits partly because it contains those data. Other causes of a difference between periods: real change in the ocean, and a different mix of instruments and regions (compare the count panel).
  • Use the count panels. A high mean cost at a depth with few data (right-hand panels) can come from one region.
  • Analysis 5 vs 6: 5 is high where there is a lot of data or poor fit; 6 removes the data-count effect. A region high in 6 but not in 5 has few, poorly fit observations.
  • Analysis 7 shows whether misfit is concentrated in latitude bands (fronts, ice edges) or depths.
  • Analysis 8: most data should lie well left of the QC line; a heavy right tail or a second peak suggests outliers.

2. Data notes

Deep XBT values are Mediterranean. In run 20261027_142247, weighted XBT T values below 2200 m are all in the Mediterranean (13 values, 2200–2800 m, T ≈ 12.9–13.9 °C), where the deep water stays near 13 °C to the bottom. Between 2000 and 2200 m, 27 of 187 values are Mediterranean. These are valid observations (the 7 values near 2400 m were within 0.01–0.31 °C of local climatology in an earlier check), but at those depths the XBT “global” mean in analysis 1 is a Mediterranean mean.

XBT values at exactly 3000 m (KI-21). 10 weighted XBT T values at exactly 3000 m, in 12 casts from two ships (1994–95, south of Tasmania and south of Japan). In WOD these casts have only coarse levels with T to 0.1 °C, and one is listed as a T-7 XBT (rated to about 760 m), so they are probably not real measurements at that depth (inferred). They are 0.6–2.7 °C warmer than local climatology; the WOD flags catch only 1 of the 12. Rule (chain.xbt_max_depth = 2900): step 06 zeroes XBT values deeper than 2900 m, which removes these 10 values and nothing else. Applied in this run.

Argo (PFL) cost below 2000 m, vs V4r6 (run 20261027_142247, true weights, unsplit). Below 2000 m only Deep Argo floats remain: 1.25 million data at 2000 m, 29 thousand at 2200 m, 10 thousand at 2600 m, in a few regions. So the mean there describes those regions, not the globe.

  • T, 2200–2400 m (cost per datum 0.93–1.03 vs about 0.64 just above): 18% of the data are in the subpolar North Atlantic (40–70°N, 60°W–0°) and carry 44% of the cost; there the model is on average 0.16 °C warmer than the observations, and the cost per datum is 2.4 against the model and 1.1 against climatology.
  • S, 2600–3000 m (0.67–0.73 vs climatology’s 0.09–0.10): 68% of the cost is in the southwest Pacific (30–45°S, 150–180°W), where the model is on average 0.016 psu saltier than the observations. The assumed error σ there is small (median 0.014 psu), so small offsets give large cost.
  • The split cost removes most of it (T split 0.25 at 2200–2400 m), so it is a steady regional offset between the model and the floats rather than variability.
  • Is climatology better only because it contains these floats? Tested: WOA23 here averages 1991–2020, so floats from 2021 on can’t be in it. Splitting the same regions by year (cost per datum against model / against climatology):
    2020 and earlier2021 and later

    Climatology still fits the newer floats far better than the model, so its good fit isn’t only from containing them. The offset is in the model: on average the model is 0.17 °C warmer (N Atlantic T) and 0.016 psu saltier (SW Pacific S) than floats from 2021 on, against 0.10 °C and 0.001 psu for climatology. For N Atlantic T, both fits get worse after 2020, which fits a change in the deep water over time (inferred). The σ values and the monthly-mean approximation also affect these numbers; not checked separately.

Near-zero cost south of 60°S. South of 60°S the chain uses a temporary constant σ of 10 °C and 5 psu (high-latitude σ rule; in this run the median Argo σ is 10 °C and 5 psu there, against 0.65 °C and 0.09 psu at |lat| < 50°), so costs there are close to zero and show as a pale ring on the maps. This is intentional for now, not missing data.

ITP outside the Arctic. In run 20261027_142247, 444 of 34,875 weighted ITP profiles are in the Southern Ocean (Weddell Sea near 74°S 33°W, Amundsen Sea near 78°S 110°W), and 4 are on the US coasts (near 42°N 71°W and 43°N 122°W; probably test casts before deployment, inferred). They show as small patches on the ITP maps.

WOD quality flags. WOD gives a quality flag for every value at its standard depths (0 = accepted, 1–9 = rejected). The UCSD input files (Interp_Profiles) don’t carry them: earlier deliveries had prof_Tflag filled with zeros, and the current files have no flag variable (confirmed by UCSD, 2026-10-26). ECCO v4r6’s files do carry them: on matching XBT casts, v4r6’s prof_Tflag equals WOD’s standard-level flags from WOD’s 2018 export 100% of the time, so the original MATLAB chain most likely took them from WOD (inferred). The chain now does the same: it reads WOD’s standard-level files from NCEI, matches each profile by its WOD cast number in prof_descr, maps the flags to our 97 depths, and step 06 zeroes the weight wherever a flag is above 0. On the same casts, 99.4% of our flags agree with v4r6’s. This applies to the WOD obs classes (CTD/OSD, XBT, mooring, glider), not to Argo, ITP or MEOP. Effect on XBT T: (ΔV/σ)² went from 0.943 (run 20261021, no flags) to 0.808 (run 20261027), against v4r6’s 0.802; the remaining gap in the final cost (0.194 vs 0.170) is in γ and sf, not in the data. Details: docs/known_issues.md KI-18, cost_comparisons/wod_flags/README.md.

4. Errata and known limitations

  • Decision (2026-10-29): the core analyses use each obs class’s own bin means for the split. Analysis 2 shows the split’s two terms, anomaly and mean, as the team asked; with each class’s own bin means both terms stay per class, which fits the specification’s per-class rule. cost_profiles.F instead pools the bin means over all obs classes; that version is kept as supplemental analysis 12 (anomaly term) and under Metadata (mean term, all classes together). To switch the core analyses to the Fortran (pooled) version: ecco-insitu profile-analyze --split pooled (or CORE_SPLIT = 'pooled' in diagnostics/profile_analyses/compute.py), then profile-report; a replot is enough, no recomputing. With pooled means the per-class mean-term line is left out of analysis 2. See also HANDOFF.md.
  • The pooled (Fortran) split (since 2026-10-28; core analyses until 2026-10-29, now supplemental analysis 12) removes one mean per (geodesic bin, depth) over all obs classes pooled, as ECCO v4r6’s cost_profiles.F, with the mean term under Metadata. Checked: for run 20261027 (all seven obs classes, vs V4r6), the anomaly cost summed over classes, its data count, the mean term and its number of terms all equal ecco-insitu model-cost’s on the same files (T: 2.87925×10⁷ over 245,226,456 data; mean term 1.96488×10⁵ over 589,586 terms). model-cost reproduces the team’s v4r6 cost bars; neither is yet checked against a model run’s own cost output. Because the pooled mean depends on which classes are included, a report made with --sources has a different pooled split.
  • Decision (2026-10-29): depth bands for the map images of analyses 5 and 6, not all 97 levels: 0–50, 50–100, 100–250, 250–500, 500–1000, 1000–2000, 2000–4000 m and deeper, plus all depths (MAP_DEPTH_BANDS in compute.py; changing them needs a re-analysis). Since 2026-10-30 the Cost map explorer (top right) draws the maps in the browser for any single level, 100 m band, 15 or 30 groups of levels, flagged cost spikes, 5-year period, and cost part, from the saved per-bin data, so the images only cover the standard bands.
  • Cost map explorer: flagged spikes. A level is flagged when its mean cost per datum (true weights, unsplit or anomaly, levels with at least 30 data) is above 0.5 and at least 1.4 times the median of the levels 300–1000 m above and below it. Broad bumps are flagged at each of their levels. These thresholds are our choice (_spikes in diagnostics/profile_analyses/viewer.py).
  • Cost map explorer: the mean-bias diagnostic (since 2026-10-31). The signed misfit, observation minus reference, averaged per bin over any depths and years, in °C or psu. It is not part of any cost, and is kept in its own row. Why: ECCO’s mean term uses the offset over the whole assimilation period (that is the window the state estimate is fit over), so a per-period mean term would not be ECCO’s; but a time-resolved offset shows drift (e.g. Argo’s North Atlantic 2200 m offset against the model grows after 2020; see Data notes). Checked: over all years it reproduces the saved per-bin bias to 1×10−6 °C.
  • Cost map explorer: precision. Values are stored to 3 significant digits (16-bit floats); values below about 6×10−5 lose precision, but they are zero on every color scale. Checked: the explorer’s data reproduce analysis 2 at the levels tested.
  • In situ data viewer (top right, since 2026-11-02): the observations themselves, not costs. A web copy of the team’s original geodesic-binning app: T and S anomalies (observation minus climatology) per geodesic bin, at any depth level and any range of years; zoomed in, one cell per profile with its true location. Only values the chain kept (weight > 0). Checked: its bin counts, means and standard deviations reproduce a direct computation from the yearly files (GLD 2017, level 1). It opens on the class shown here, and its ← report button comes back; its own Guide lists every difference from the original app.
  • Two small differences from model-cost: profiles without a geodesic bin (about 14,000 of 441 million weighted values in run 20261027_142247) have no split cost here; and data whose climatology is missing are left out of all costs here, while ECCO keeps them in the model cost (none in these runs).
  • Split vs climatology is our extension; ECCO splits only the model–data cost.
  • Analysis 4: the specification asks for total cost through time “after all factors removed”; that is the true line. The final and nosf lines are shown too.
  • The model costs are approximate: Vestim comes from monthly means, so sub-monthly variability shows up as misfit; profiles after December 2025 have no model value.
  • Corrections to earlier versions of this report (before 2026-10-28): the depth note said levels with fewer than 30 observations were hidden (only levels with none are); the histogram text said 2 means 100σ (it means 10σ); the WOD-flags note said a flag of 1 means good (0 means accepted) and listed Argo and MEOP among the WOD classes (they aren’t); the split was described as mirroring cost_profiles.F (until 2026-10-28 it used each obs class’s own mean, now split_own, and left out the mean term); and the map bins with no data were drawn as zero cost (now blank).
  • Analysis 1 changed on 2026-10-28. It used to average only the data that had a reference value for the selected cost type (the same data as the costs), so it differed between the cost-type tabs: under vs V4r6 it left out every datum with no model value. Measured on run 20261027_142247: CTD 14% fewer data under vs V4r6 (mean T differed by up to 0.64 °C, at 40 m), gliders 4% (0.13 °C), XBT 3% (0.17 °C, at 2200 m), Argo 1% (0.02 °C). It now averages every observation with weight > 0 and is the same in every tab. The old version shows the observations behind each cost type’s costs, so it may be worth having again. To revert: in diagnostics/profile_analyses/compute.py, _accumulate_file, accumulate the a01_* sums with the cost mask ok instead of obs, and drop analysis 01 from NO_COST in report.py.

What the difference means. The gap between pooled and own is the extra cost from a class’s own mean misfit differing from the shared one, i.e. its offset relative to the other instruments in the same bins. In both runs pooled ≥ own for every class (expected but not guaranteed, since the cost is weighted and the mean is not).

  • Argo (PFL) differs by about 1%: Argo has most of the data, so the pooled mean is largely Argo’s own.
  • ITP differs by 1–2%, probably because few other classes sample its Arctic bins (inferred). Moorings: 2–6%.
  • CTD, gliders, XBT and MEOP differ most: 5–17% against the model and 7–21% against climatology, MEOP salinity the most. Their mean misfit departs from the shared one (inferred: instrument offsets, or sampling different seasons and years within a bin).
  • For most classes the gap is larger against climatology than against the model.

The percentages above are from these two runs; the causes are inferences and haven’t been checked.

Terminology

Weights and σ. Every observation (one value of T or S at one depth level) has its own weight w, and its cost is w · (ΔV)². ECCO’s cost is the sum over all observations. Every cost in this report is a mean of that per-observation cost (over the data at a depth, in a month, in a bin), except the split’s mean term, which is one value per bin. The processing chain builds w = γ · sf / σ² in three steps:

StepFactorWhat it is
041/σ²σ is the assumed error of the observation: how far from the reference it is expected to be, not the instrument’s accuracy (Argo sensors: about 0.002 °C; σ for T here: about 0.9 °C near the surface, 0.25 °C at 500–1000 m, 0.1 °C at depth). It is a derived field, a map per depth (see below).
10γThe area of the profile’s model grid cell over the largest cell’s area (0–1), so that small high-latitude cells don’t count more than large ones.
11sfThe spatial scaling factor (0–1, about 1/n for n profiles in a geodesic bin and month), so that densely sampled places and times don’t dominate the cost.

Where σ comes from. Between 60°S and 60°N: the ECCO uncertainty fields on the model grid (Theta_/Salt_sigma_smoothed_method_02_masked_merged_capped_extrapolated.bin, the files of the original MATLAB chain; 50 levels, interpolated to each profile’s depths). North of 60°N: an Arctic file made for this chain, the expected RMS difference between Arctic observations and the climatology (bias included), estimated per 100 km cell from 1992–2023 observations and smoothed toward its neighbors. South of 60°S: for now, a constant (10 °C, 5 psu), which makes those observations count very little. S has a floor of 0.005 psu.

Weight versions: the figures show the cost with three choices of w. All use the same misfit ΔV = V − Vref, squared, so its sign doesn’t matter here. Analysis 2 draws all three; analysis 9 is its true lines alone.

LabelCost per datumMeaning
finalγ · sf · (ΔV/σ)² As ECCO weights it: area factor γ and step-11 spatial scaling factor sf both kept
nosfγ · (ΔV/σ)² sf removed, γ kept
true(ΔV/σ)² γ and sf removed: “cost per datum” (all factors removed)

γ and sf are both between 0 and 1, so true ≥ nosf ≥ final. Example: PFL T against the model at 240 m: 0.19 (final), 0.68 (nosf), 1.04 (true). final is small because sf and γ shrink the weights, not because the fit is better; true is the one to read as “how many σ off”.
